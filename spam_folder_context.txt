"Music from the Spam Folder" collides one of the most mundane, easily ignored facets of modern digital life — junk mail (spam) — with the possibilities  offered by state-of-the-art prompt-based A.I. music generation. Sturm chooses the more violent term “collide” rather than “combine” to emphasize his forcing of spam into lyrics, and then his forcing of these into A.I. music generation systems.  The procedure is reminiscent of “song poems”,  or rather the trolling of such services  — not to mention a somewhat passive aggressive trolling of spammers.  What can prompt-based A.I. music systems trained on a lot of the world’s recorded music — the exact amount is currently unknown  — make from spam? Is the result “spam music”? The album is available on bandcamp.
Through this work Sturm has found that his spam, often dismissed as clutter, noise, or worse, reflects his existence. Each track on the album uses a different piece of spam (as a starting point, and is composed from audio data generated by prompt-based A.I.. For some tracks, Sturm leaves the audio unmodified (save for equalization); and for others he fractures it and composes with the pieces. In either case, the approach can be likened to a kind of “pseudoplunderphonics”:  the plundering is not exactly direct, but once removed by a machine learning pipeline processing a large dataset of plundered music audio.
The tracks on "Music from the Spam Folder" explore themes of globalization, identity and nostalgia, insecurity, paranoia and catastrophe, loneliness and desire, language barriers, and the impersonal yet deeply invasive nature of spam. The result is a personal album that is playful and provocative, but also disturbing and beautiful, highlighting the absurdity and the latent beauty of the digital ephemera automatically filtered from our inboxes, as well as reflecting particulars of Sturm as a person (e.g., mind, body, status, occupation and politics), and demonstrating the state-of-the-art in A.I. music generation technology — the abilities of which Sturm finds totally disconcerting. Sturm neither anticipated that the resulting music would make him experience feels for his spam and their authors (empathy arising from how these A.I. systems respond musically to the various texts forced upon them), nor that so much of the resulting music is actually (unfortunately maybe?) catchy.
Sturm is wary of taking too much credit, however. He believes that to create the music on this album “from the blank page” is maybe beyond his abilities — or certainly beyond the time and patience that he has to devote to his vision of the album. Udio and Suno have carried most of the burden in those respects, and Sturm is not sure how to feel about that. He does feel rather like someone making a mixtape — is this really composing? A way to resolve this dilemma is to think of the music on this album as a result of a process. Anyone can thus make this music:
1.  Pick a piece of spam.
2.  Into a prompt-based A.I. music generation system copy and paste the spam as lyrics, and enter some description of the music.
3.  Click “create” (or equivalent).
4.  Download the results and further edit in the D.A.W. of your choice (or keep as is).
5.  Share and reflect (or don’t).
